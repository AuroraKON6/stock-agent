# Thesis And Catalyst Ledger

Maintain a research ledger, not a position ledger.

1. Express the core hypothesis in one testable sentence and split it into three
   to five measurable pillars.
2. For every update, record event date, evidence date, supplied evidence
   reference, affected pillar, expected observation, actual observation, and
   whether the evidence supports, weakens, invalidates, or leaves it unresolved.
3. Track confirming and disconfirming evidence with equal discipline. Do not let
   repeated reports of the same origin count as independent confirmation.
4. Record catalysts with expected date range, event type, expected evidence,
   dependency, status, actual outcome, and next verification time. Re-check dates
   because company and regulatory calendars can change.
5. State explicit invalidation conditions and unresolved assumptions. Lower
   confidence when a catalyst passed without the expected observable result.
6. Route material thesis changes to human review with concrete questions.

Allowed statuses are supported, weakening, unresolved, or invalidated. Do not
output long or short direction, target price, stop loss, sizing, or pre-positioning.
