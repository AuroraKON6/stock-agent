# A-share Disclosure Analysis

Use only supplied public documents and metadata. Preserve the caller's output
schema and separate quoted facts, management assertions, model inference, and
unknowns.

1. Classify the document: periodic report, performance forecast, contract,
   acquisition, disposal, repurchase, holding change, litigation, penalty,
   inquiry letter, response, correction, or other filing.
2. Extract issuer, counterparties, amount, ratio, period, approval state,
   conditions precedent, effective date, performance obligations, and disclosed
   uncertainty. Keep units and accounting scope explicit.
3. Link corrections, supplements, inquiry letters, and responses to the earlier
   disclosure. Report what changed, what was answered, and what remains open.
4. Compare material language and figures with prior supplied filings. Flag
   changed definitions, delayed milestones, missing attachments, and revised
   risk language.
5. Attach exact supplied evidence references to every material claim. A headline
   or secondary summary alone cannot establish a P0 or P1 fact.
6. Produce research implications as hypotheses, not trade instructions. Include
   follow-up dates, falsification evidence, and questions for a human analyst.

Never invent an original announcement, filing URL, amount, date, approval, or
regulatory conclusion.
